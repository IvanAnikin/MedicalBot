Patient is Sarah Mitchell, a 34-year-old female, date of birth June 22nd 1991. She is presenting with persistent cough for the past 10 days. She reports the cough started as dry but has now become productive with yellowish-green sputum. She also complains of low-grade fever, measured at home as 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit, along with fatigue and mild shortness of breath with exertion. She denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or wheezing. She tried over-the-counter cough suppressants and honey with lemon tea without significant improvement. She works as an elementary school teacher and mentions several children in her class have been sick recently. Past medical history is unremarkable, no chronic conditions. She takes a daily multivitamin and oral contraceptive pills. No known drug allergies. Non-smoker, occasional alcohol use. On physical examination, temperature 100.2 degrees Fahrenheit, blood pressure 118 over 74, heart rate 78, respiratory rate 18, oxygen saturation 97 percent on room air. Oropharynx shows mild erythema with postnasal drip. Cervical lymph nodes are slightly enlarged and tender bilaterally. Lung auscultation reveals scattered rhonchi in the right lower lobe that partially clear with coughing. No wheezing or crackles. Heart sounds normal. Assessment: Acute bronchitis, likely viral etiology with possible early bacterial superinfection given the prolonged course and purulent sputum. Plan: Prescribe azithromycin 500 milligrams day one then 250 milligrams days two through five given the duration and sputum changes. Benzonatate 100 milligrams three times daily as needed for cough. Push oral fluids and rest. Use humidifier at night. Return in one week if not improving or sooner if developing high fever, worsening shortness of breath, or hemoptysis. Consider chest X-ray if symptoms persist beyond two weeks.
